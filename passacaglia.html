Passacaglia

One ground, twenty variations, four voices, and what passes between them.

The ground is eight bars and fifteen notes in the bass, stated twenty-one times, and the rest of the piece is written over it. On the wheel the ground and the twenty variations run round the face in the order they play, with the one in play at the top. Inside, the ground is the black ring, and the voices of the variation in play turn round it, the cell in bold and the rest light. In XI to XV, hollow on the wheel, the pedal falls silent and the ground moves up into the voices.

Scroll to read. Tap the middle of the wheel to play, or a variation to go there.

The cells

Each variation is built on one cell, a figure of four or five notes with a fixed rhythm and shape. The view shows one variation at a time, with its cell at the left and the voices across its eight bars in the block, each voice on its own lane and the readings beneath. The solid notes are the cell. Where a cell is handed from voice to voice, each statement that another voice takes up is drawn in red, and where two voices carry it together, as alto and tenor do in I, II, and XVIII, both are drawn solid.

Tap a voice to hear it alone, or the block for all of them. The page follows the playing, with the wheel and the block together.
