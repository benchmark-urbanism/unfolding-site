Truchet

One tile: a square cut on its diagonal, one half dark. Tap it, and it turns.

Scroll to read. Tap the board at any point.

Four turns

The tile has four orientations, one for each corner the dark half can hold.

In 1704 Sébastien Truchet, a priest and engineer, laid them side by side and asked how many patterns they make.

Any arrangement

A board of 144 tiles, each in one of four orientations, has about 5 × 1086 arrangements, more than there are atoms in the observable universe.

Tap a tile to turn it. Hold it, or tap with shift, to swap its colours.

Blocks of four

Truchet catalogued his patterns by the small block that repeats: four tiles, each turned, and the four repeated across the board.

Choose a block. Then turn one tile, and each copy of it turns with it, so four choices set the board.

Smith's arcs

In 1987 Cyril Stanley Smith published Truchet's memoir in Pauline Boucher's translation, with an essay of his own. Smith was a metallurgist who spent his working life on the structure of metals. Much of a metal's behaviour depends on how neighbouring crystals meet at their boundaries. He read the memoir as an early study of the same question, how a tile that meets its neighbours by a simple rule gives rise to a larger design.

He also drew a second tile: two quarter-circles, each joining the middles of two neighbouring edges. It has two orientations, and each arc meets an arc on the next tile.

Curves across the board

The arcs join into curves that run across the board. Some close into loops, some run to the edge. Each curve is coloured here by its length, and the loops are warm.

Each curve is made by the choices of many tiles together. Turn one tile, and a curve splits or two join.

The next scale

Take the tiles four at a time. A block of four can be filled in 256 ways. Outlined, the blocks are the pieces of the next scale up.

The count at the top right says how many of the 256 the board uses. A block of four can sit in 121 places on this board, and a random board uses a different block in most of them. One block repeated uses four.

Anni Albers

Anni Albers drew triangles on graph paper from 1959, hundreds of sheets of them, and made prints from them: the Triangulated Intaglios of 1976 and the portfolio Connections of 1983.

A grid square cut on its diagonal into a dark and a light triangle is Truchet's tile. Two side by side make a pyramid, the form she returned to.

Start from one of these arrangements, of the kinds she drew, and change it.

The board takes a palette after her prints: black, a red, and a grey on paper. Tap a tile to turn it, and hold it to change its colour or leave it empty. Make a variant of your own.
